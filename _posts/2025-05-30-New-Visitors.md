## We Are So Excited to Have New Faces Joining the Team This Year!

### Welcome Mac to the Lab!
<!-- starting **July 1st** -->

We’re excited to welcome Mac to the lab as he joins us for his postdoctoral work where he will bring his expertise in evolutionary genomics and host–microbiome dynamics to the team. Mac holds an M.S. in Biology from the University of Colorado Denver and recently completed his Ph.D. in Evolutionary Biology at the University of Pennsylvania. His doctoral research focused on the genomics of seasonality and diapause in *Rhagoletis pomonella*, and more recently, he has been exploring plant–microbe interactions at the molecular level. Welcome to the team!


### Welcome Amartya to the Lab!
<!-- starting **May 20** -->

We’re happy to welcome **Amartya** to the lab as a visiting undergraduate researcher for this summer.

Amartya is a sophomore at the University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign, majoring in Integrative Biology Honors with a minor in Statistics. His research explores plant–microbe coevolution, including experiments using barcoded Sinorhizobium strains in *Medicago truncatula*. We're excited to welcome him to the lab this summer!


### Welcome Michael to the Lab!
<!-- starting **June 9** -->

We’re excited to welcome **Michael** to the lab for the summer!

Michael earned his undergraduate degree in Biochemistry and Molecular Biology from the University of Dhaka and his PhD at the Arizona Genomics Institute in the School of Plant Sciences. This summer, he’ll be collaborating with Hanna on the comparative R gene project, focusing on polyploid species such as Draba verna. His work centers on genome assembly as part of the lab’s broader efforts to understand the evolution and function of plant immune genes.

We’re thrilled to have him on board!

### Welcome Em to the Lab!
<!-- starting **April** -->

We’re excited to welcome **Em**, who joined the lab as a Laboratory Manager this spring!

Em earned a B.A. in Biology from Occidental College, focusing on zoology, and completed an M.S. in Biology with a concentration in bioinformatics and systems biology at New York University. Their graduate work included analyzing the population decline of *Phoenix theophrasti* and contributing to Alzheimer’s research at NYU Langone, helping to develop new diagnostic and treatment strategies. They will be supporting the lab with administrative responsibilities and collaborating with postdoctoral researchers on computational biology projects.

Welcome, Em!
