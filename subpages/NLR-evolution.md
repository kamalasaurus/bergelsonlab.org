<div class="research-grid-container">
    <div class="research-grid-item research-grid-span-2">The evolution of NLR (nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat) genes in <em>Arabidopsis thaliana</em> and its relatives reflects adaptation to microbial threats, with these resistance (R) genes key to detecting pathogen effectors and triggering immunity. We investigate the ecological, genomic, and functional drivers of R gene evolution by studying NLR-effector interactions across global and local populations.</div>
    <div class="research-grid-item">
        <img src="../assets/images/NLR-Image2.jpg" alt="Placeholder Image" />
    </div>
    <div class="research-grid-item">
    <p>
      Despite high levels of molecular variation in selfing species like <em>Arabidopsis thaliana</em>, R genes—particularly NB-LRR types—exhibit striking polymorphism, often maintained as ancient balanced variants. These genes, typically found in complex, duplicated loci, are central to plant immunity but remain challenging to analyze due to their structural complexity. One component of this project aims to determine whether orthologous R genes across <em>A. thaliana</em>, <em>Cardamine hirsuta</em>, and <em>Erophila verna</em> share evolutionary histories, retain conserved functions, and are similarly integrated within the broader NLR immune signaling network. In a parallel effort, we investigate natural variation in NLR genes across <em>Arabidopsis thaliana</em>, <em>Cardamine hirsuta</em>, and <em>Draba verna</em>a from natural populations in the Midi-Pyrénées region of southern France to assess whether these genes reflect shared evolutionary origins and ecological roles. Variation in LRR domains, patterns of presence/absence, and levels of constraint among NLRs reveal distinct evolutionary strategies that influence not only pathogen resistance but also interactions with plant-associated microbial communities, providing insight into how immune gene diversity is shaped across species and environments.
      
    </p>
